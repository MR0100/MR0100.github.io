Concept prototype · Temu case study

Keep the fun, fix the contract: shopping games that play fair

Temu’s games do real work: a reason to open the app daily, cheap growth through referrals, and browsing that turns into buying. In 450 App Store reviews, players enjoy them. The anger is about the rules: in the 111 reviews about the games, 17 say progress crawls near the end and 14 say “free” turned into spend. This keeps the spin, the celebration and the countdowns, and makes the rules visible, fixed and true.

Independent study by Mitul Vaghasiya, not affiliated with Temu. Every screen below is my re-creation in Temu’s visual style, based on its App Store screenshots (10 Oct 2026) and the public images listed in references/. They are not screenshots of the real app.

What I’d build instead

The same moment, in 4 screens.

1

Same wheel, rules on it

It still opens, lights up and spins. The chips say every spin wins, €5 to €20, use on €15+, no invites. Skip and “don’t show on open” are there.

Informed choice
2

The win still celebrates

Confetti and a big “€10 off”. The 48-hour countdown is real, from the server, and the credit is already in the account.

Honest urgency
3

Steps that never shrink

Six steps, each worth the same: check in, look at deals, order, invite any friend. It shows the finish date and last month’s finish rate.

Goal-gradient, used fairly
4

It pays out what it said

The order adds its 2 steps and unlocks the mug. The late-delivery credit, the most-praised reward in the reviews, is promised up front.

Fairness and trust
What to take from this

Three lessons for any app

  1. Find out what a feature does for the business before you redesign it. Removing the fun would have removed the habit.
  2. Read the five-star reviews too. They tell you what to keep.
  3. Keep the urgency and the celebration. Make every rule visible before the tap, and make progress worth the same at every step.
What the user test will decide

Does B stay as fun as A while feeling fairer? If 3 of 5 testers find it clearly less exciting, or stop the gift game once they see the real cost, the reward itself needs rethinking.
