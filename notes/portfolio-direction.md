# Portfolio direction: research and recommendation (10 Oct 2026)

Why: the first live version reads like a research blog (2,000–2,900-word articles with 4 tables each, a subscribe sidebar, a marketing bar). Mitul wants a neat, clean, professional portfolio that shows 7+ years of experience at a glance, led by the work.

## What hiring managers and design leads say
- **Fewer, deeper projects: 3–5.** "Quality over quantity is the best rule." (NN/g, Rachel Krause, 2019, from a survey of 204 hiring managers) https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-design-portfolios/
- **They scan.** "Very rarely will hiring managers take the time to read your entire portfolio word for word." Break up text with visuals; "resist the urge to go overboard on a flashy template." (NN/g)
- **Each case study:** problem, your role, how you got there, how it solved the problem, challenges and abandoned ideas, impact on users and business, what you learned. (NN/g)
- **Short blocks.** "3–5 sentences (not multiple paragraphs)" per description; say "I", not "we"; show craft at every level. (Leslie Yang, design manager, 2023) https://leslieyang.substack.com/p/what-a-hiring-manager-looks-for-in
- **Shipped beats concept.** Managers value shipped products and iteration with metrics; "the portfolio is king"; first question: does it look modern? (Indeed Design, 2020) https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/
- **Seniority = scope and judgement, not process.** Senior portfolios are judged on scope, influence and decisions under constraint (secondary summaries of NN/g and practitioner guides; treat as directional).
- **NDA work:** recreate designs in a generic style, remove logos, blur sensitive data, show process artefacts, or describe at a high level; a couple of concept projects are fine. (NN/g; UX Playbook https://uxplaybook.org/articles/ux-design-portfolio-nda-guide-2026)

## What real senior portfolios do (studied 10 Oct 2026)
Two archetypes:
1. **Text-first minimal** (Brian Lovin, Emil Kowalski, Paco Coursey, Karri Saarinen): a name, one line, lists. Works because their reputation already sells them. Not right for us yet.
2. **Work-led** (Austin Knight, Zoë Sinner): the model to follow.
   - Hero: one confident sentence + a credentials line ("Previously at Hinge Health, Facebook & Oculus").
   - Selected work: 2–3 large cards, each a real device mockup on a calm background, with company, role and years.
   - About: short bio, experience timeline (years · company · role), personal photos.
   - Testimonials ("In their words"), big simple contact line, résumé PDF.
   - Experience is legible from the layout before any reading.

Case-study page pattern (Zoë Sinner, Karolis Kosas, Simon Pan, Elise Fu):
- Header: outcome-framed title + one-sentence summary on the left; a metadata column on the right (Role · Timeline · Platform · Outcome · Team).
- Full-width hero mockup.
- Sections with small eyebrow labels (Context · Problem · Approach · Key decisions · Outcome) and a headline that states the point ("Fix the foundation first, then introduce the new"), then 2–4 short paragraphs.
- Narrow text column, wide images; lots of white space.
- The outcome as one big statement ("From 6.3 sessions a week to 10.2"); a quote; what's next.
- Results either first (Elise Fu, Madeline Wukusick) or last with a clear "Impact" section (Simon Pan). Role stated early everywhere.

## Diagnosis of our v1
| Signal | v1 | Should be |
|---|---|---|
| First 10 seconds | Long headline + list of article titles | Name, role, one line, credentials, big work visuals |
| Case study length | 1,850–2,850 words, 4 tables each | ~700–1,000 words, mostly visuals; full research as an optional appendix |
| Visuals | Single phone screenshots inline | Consistent device mockups in scenes, before/after pairs, one hero per study |
| Experience | Not visible | Timeline, years, 35+ apps, team lead, real outcomes |
| Shipped work | None shown (all concept studies) | Real shipped/NDA work first, studies second, clearly labelled |
| Tone | Research article (tags, sidebars, subscribe bar) | Calm, editorial, confident |

## Recommendation
**Information architecture**
- **Home (one scroll, scannable in 10 s):** hero → selected work (4 cards: 2 shipped/NDA, 2 studies) → impact strip (7+ yrs · 35+ apps · +30% engagement · 99.9% uptime) → experience timeline → short about + photo → testimonials → latest writing → contact.
- **Work:** Shipped work (anonymised NDA case studies) · Independent studies · App gallery (the 35+ apps as icons/cards where allowed).
- **Case study template (new):** header + metadata column → hero mockup → TL;DR (problem / what I did / outcome) → 4–5 labelled sections with claim headlines → key decisions as before/after → outcome as a big statement → reflection → "Go deeper": live prototype, full study, test kit, sources.
- **About:** story, how I work (Validate first), principles, experience timeline, stack and skills, photos, résumé PDF.
- **Writing:** LinkedIn posts as short articles, then the newsletter.
- **Contact:** in the footer and on About.

**Visual direction**
- Light, warm off-white, generous whitespace, one display serif for headlines + a clean sans for text, one accent colour, no badges in the main flow.
- One consistent mockup system: same device frame and angle, each project with its own soft background colour.
- Big numbers for outcomes; small uppercase eyebrows; 680px text column, 1200px image width.

**Content needed from Mitul**
1. Photo(s): one portrait, 2–3 informal.
2. Shipped work: which of the 35+ apps can be shown (name, store link, role, years, platforms) and how much of the KGK work the NDAs allow (name? metrics? anonymised screens?).
3. Testimonials: 2–3 lines from colleagues or clients (LinkedIn recommendations are fine).
4. A one-page résumé (or permission to make one from the LinkedIn facts in `about-me.md`).
