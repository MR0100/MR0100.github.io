<p class="eyebrow">The question changed</p>

## Half right about the paywall

The barcode scanner went behind Premium in 2022, and people still mention it. But counting 145 low-rated reviews showed a larger group: 42 people said the 2026 redesign made logging or reviewing their day slower, many of them five- to ten-year users.

<p class="eyebrow">What the evidence shows</p>

## Many small logging moments beat long ones

In a 24-week trial, people who reached 5% weight loss opened their food journal 2.4 times a day, against 1.6 for those who didn’t, and time spent logging didn’t predict success. The goal is cheap, frequent logging. The tools for repeat meals already exist in the app; reviewers just find them hard to reach.

<p class="eyebrow">The design</p>

## Speed for repeats doesn’t need AI

The category is racing to photo logging, but only 6 of the 145 reviews mention AI at all. My version uses something the app already has, the user’s own history: a time-aware “usual meal” card, edits in place, your foods first, and copying that keeps meals in their slots.

<p class="eyebrow">How I’ll know</p>

## Faster logging, without killing the upgrade

Five people log the same breakfast on both versions, and I count taps and seconds. The risk to test is the business one: if repeats cover most meals, will fewer people see a reason to upgrade?
