<p class="eyebrow">The question changed</p>

## From “is the streak too much?” to “what happens when it breaks?”

Streaks are often blamed for anxiety, so that was my starting point. Counting the complaints changed it. Of 127 one- and two-star reviews, 9 were about streaks, and 8 of those came from people trying to keep one, some at 900 or 1,000 days, losing it to bugs, used-up freezes or a lack of gems.

<p class="eyebrow">What Duolingo already proved</p>

## The demand is real, and it was open for one month

In June 2026 Duolingo let lapsed learners bring back a lost streak by finishing three lessons. 15.4 million did, and the company told investors they retained better than a typical returning group. Then the offer ended. Research agrees: people are less likely to continue after a streak breaks, and the effect is weaker when they can repair it.

<p class="eyebrow">The design</p>

## A pause, a short plan, and an honest record

The streak pauses instead of resetting. Earning it back takes three short sessions, so the number still means practice. Day 1 is an energy-free warm-up built from the learner’s weakest words, because a returning learner has high intent and little to spend. The restored day keeps its own mark, so it’s fair to people who never broke theirs.

<p class="eyebrow">How I’ll know</p>

## The risk is urgency

A safety net could make people practise less on busy days. The test asks exactly that, and the production experiment would keep a long-running holdout to see whether daily practice drops among learners who never broke their streak.
