Liquid Glass arrived at WWDC in June 2025: see-through bars and buttons that bend whatever sits behind them.

It looked stunning in the keynote. On real phones, many people struggled to read it:
- Text on glass loses contrast over busy photos
- Glass stacked on glass
- Bright sunlight makes it worse

Apple fixed it in steps:
- Summer 2025 betas: more solid navigation bars
- iOS 26.1: a switch to tone the glass down
- iOS 27 (WWDC 2026): less transparent by default, plus a slider from clear to tinted

That isn't a failure. It's the most expensive public usability test of the year, and Apple listened.

What I'm taking from it for my own apps:
1. Contrast before effects. Body text needs at least a 4.5:1 contrast ratio.
2. Glass is for controls, not for reading.
3. Test outside in sunlight, and with Reduce Transparency switched on.
