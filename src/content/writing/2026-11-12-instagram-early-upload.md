That's one reason posting felt instant in its early days.

The usual way:
Pick a photo → add a filter → write a caption → tap Share → wait.

Instagram's way, as its co-founders have described it:
- The upload started in the background while you were still editing
- Photos were small back then (512 × 512), so it usually finished in seconds
- By the time you'd written your caption, the slow part was done
- You tapped Share, and the progress bar just flew

And if you cancelled? The uploaded data was thrown away. Co-founder Mike Krieger said that was worth it.

The principle: do the slow work while people are busy with something else.

Where it fits in your app:
- Start uploading a profile photo as soon as it's picked
- Load the next screen while people read the current one
- Check a form field while they type the next one

The trade-off is real: you spend data and server work on things people might cancel. For the moments they care about most, it's usually worth it.
