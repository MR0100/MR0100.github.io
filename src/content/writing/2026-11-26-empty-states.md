Think about the first time you open a to-do app, an inbox or a dashboard. There's nothing in it yet.

That empty screen is often the first real screen a new user sees. And too many apps fill it with two words: "No data".

A good empty state does three things:
1. It says what will appear here. "Your saved recipes will live here."
2. It gives one clear action. "Save your first recipe", not five buttons.
3. It shows an example, so people can picture it working.

Even better: a sample item people can try, then delete. It teaches the app without a tour.

Empty states aren't an edge case. For every new user, they're the first impression.
