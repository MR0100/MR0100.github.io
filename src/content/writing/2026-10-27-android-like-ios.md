I hear this request every month. Here's why it's usually a mistake.

Users don't compare your Android app with your iOS app.
They compare it with every other app on their phone.

What breaks when you copy iOS onto Android:
- Back: Android users expect the system back gesture everywhere, not just a top-left arrow
- Sharing: Android's share sheet works differently, and fighting it feels broken
- Dates & pickers: an iOS wheel on Android feels foreign
- Small details: switches, dialogs and fonts all feel slightly "off"

The result: the app feels foreign on half of your users' phones.

What I recommend instead:
- Same brand: colours, type, tone, icons
- Native behaviour: navigation, gestures, system sheets

With Flutter it's tempting to build one identical UI. Being able to doesn't mean you should.

Are you in the "one design for both" camp, or "native on each"? Why?
