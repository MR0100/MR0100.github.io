"Would you use an app that…?" is the most useless question in product research. People say yes to be nice. Then they never download it.

I'm interviewing people for a product idea right now, and the rule I follow is simple: ask about the past, not the future.

5 rewrites:
✗ "Would you use this?"
✓ "When did you last have this problem?"

✗ "How much would you pay?"
✓ "What do you use today, and what does it cost you?"

✗ "Is this a good idea?"
✓ "What did you try last time? What happened?"

✗ "Would this feature help?"
✓ "Walk me through the last time you did this."

✗ "Do you like the design?"
✓ "What would you do first on this screen?"

Listen for time and money people have already spent. That's the real signal.

These come from The Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick, a short book worth reading before any user interview.
