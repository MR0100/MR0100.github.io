The European Accessibility Act now applies to many apps and websites that serve people in the EU, including those made by companies outside Europe:
- Online shops
- Banking and payments
- Transport tickets and travel
- E-books
- Phone, messaging and TV services

The smallest companies get a break: service providers with fewer than 10 people and up to €2 million a year are exempt.

Enforcement differs by country, and I'm not a lawyer. But the checklist is the same one good apps should already pass.

6 checks worth running on any app:
1. Text grows with the phone's font size setting, without breaking the layout.
2. Text has at least 4.5:1 contrast with its background.
3. Every button has a label a screen reader can say. "Button" isn't a label.
4. Tap targets are at least 44 pt on iOS and 48 dp on Android.
5. Animations calm down when Reduce Motion (iOS) or Remove animations (Android) is on.
6. Videos have captions.

None of this is only for people with disabilities. Bigger text, clearer buttons and captions help everyone: in sunlight, on a bus, with one hand.
