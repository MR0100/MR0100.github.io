It's been standard advice for over 20 years: small tests find most of the problems. Yet most teams I've worked with have never run one.

The kit I use for every case study:
1. Write two sentences first: what result ships it, and what result kills it.
2. Recruit five people who do the real job. Not colleagues.
3. A prototype that behaves: today's version and the new one.
4. Alternate the order (A→B, B→A…), because whatever people see second gains from practice.
5. Watch what they do: finished or not, taps, time, where they hesitate.
6. Fill in the results the same day, even if they kill the idea.

What five people can't do: tell you how big a problem is. That's what a staged rollout is for. The test tells you whether it's worth building and what to fix first.

What's the last design decision you wish you'd tested first?
