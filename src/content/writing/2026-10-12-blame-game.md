After 7 years and 35+ apps, I think everyone is blaming the wrong thing.

When an app fails, the post-mortem sounds like this:
- "The design wasn't practical."
- "The developers didn't follow the design."
- "The team was too slow."

But most of the failed apps I've seen weren't badly designed or badly built.

They were well designed and well built…
for a problem users didn't actually have.

The real culprit is skipping one step: finding out what users need before anyone opens Figma or writes code.

That's how I work now:
- Research: talk to users, read the data and the reviews
- Validate: prototype and test before it becomes expensive code
- Build & measure: ship, then check if people actually stay

I'm a Senior Mobile Developer and Team Lead. Here I'll share teardowns, validation stories and the mistakes I've learned from.

When a product fails, who gets blamed first at your company, and is it ever the right person?
