Floating glass tab bars. Round buttons in the corners. A "‹ Back" in the top left.

Since Apple's Liquid Glass launched, Android reviewers have been calling out apps that copy iOS styling onto Android.

I get why teams do it. One design, two platforms, half the work.

But Android users feel it, even if they can't say why:
- Back: Android already has a system back gesture. A second back button in the corner competes with it.
- Navigation: iOS-style floating bars ignore Android's own patterns and edge-to-edge screens.
- Controls: iOS-style pickers, dialogs and switches behave differently from what Android users expect.
- Sharing: a home-made share menu instead of Android's own share sheet hides the apps people actually use.

The fix isn't two separate designs. It's splitting what's yours from what belongs to the platform.

Yours: colours, icons, illustrations, words, the core flows.
The platform's: navigation, back, system controls, gestures, sharing.

Share the brand. Not the costume.
