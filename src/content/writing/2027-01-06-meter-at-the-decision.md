1 was about finding a feature.
54 were about usage limits.

People found the photo tools, started relying on them, and hit a wall mid-task. The remaining allowance existed, but it lived in Settings, two screens away from where they chose the tool.

Limits don't lose users. Surprises do.

Four rules I'd apply to any limit or paywall:
1. Show the meter where people choose ("Photos · 3 left today").
2. Warn before the last one, with the reset time.
3. Give more than one way out: save for later, do it another way, or upgrade.
4. Never lose the work.

The real risk: a visible meter might make people use less. So I wrote the kill signal first: if 3 of 5 testers hold back because they see the count, I rethink.

Where does your product say "you've run out"?
