Duolingo's best comeback feature lasted one month. A broken streak still has no permanent way back.

Disclaimer. This is an independent product-design study by Mitul Vaghasiya. I am not affiliated with Duolingo, and Duolingo has not endorsed this study. I have no access to internal data. Every number links to a public source. Anything I could not verify is marked Hypothesis. Screens are public App Store screenshots and a press image supplied by Duolingo. I keep them here for research only. On the public portfolio page I will re-capture or redraw them.

When a Duolingo streak breaks, the counter goes back to zero. The ways to undo that (Streak Freeze and Streak Repair) cost gems, and the first lessons back cost energy. In June 2026 Duolingo ran a one-time "Streak Revival" event. 15.4 million learners took it, nearly 8 million of them with no active streak, and Duolingo says these users retained better than a typical re-engaged cohort. That is strong evidence of a large, recoverable group of lapsed learners. My proposal is to turn that one-off campaign into a permanent "earn it back" path that checks skills as well as restoring the number.

Why this app

  • Scale. Q2 2026: 58.7M daily active users (+23% YoY), 140.6M monthly active users and 12.7M paid subscribers (Q2 2026 shareholder letter). Revenue for the quarter was $298.5M (same source).
  • Business model. Freemium. The free tier has ads and an Energy limit. Super and Max subscribers get unlimited energy, according to press coverage of the rollout (feedbagel summary of the change). The CEO said on the Q2 2026 call that a new "Super Lite" tier gives "twice as much energy" (Q2 2026 call transcript, Webull).
  • The metric that matters: retention, specifically CURR. Duolingo defines CURR as "the proportion of 'current user DAUs' who come back the next day". It was at "an all-time high of 84%" in Q2 2026, and Duolingo calls it "one of our most important leading indicators" (Q2 2026 letter). Former CPO Jorge Mazal wrote that moving CURR had about 5x the effect on DAU of the next-best lever (Lenny's Newsletter, Feb 2023).

Duolingo's streak story is well known. Less is written about what happens to the people who fall out of the current-user bucket when their streak breaks. Mazal's growth model counts them as "reactivated" (back after 7–29 days away) or "resurrected" (back after 30+ days) (same source). This study is about that moment.

The problem

Who is affected: learners who built a habit and then missed a day without a Streak Freeze equipped. Long-streak users are affected most, because they have the most to lose.

What goes wrong: the streak resets to zero. Duolingo's own blog says losing a streak can feel "quite demotivating", and that fear of losing one can stop people from starting one (Duolingo blog, Jan 2022). Reporting on Duolingo's June 2026 announcement says "losing a streak is also a common reason some users stop returning to the platform" (Marketech APAC, 3 Jun 2026). Restoring lost streaks was one of the most requested features: "tens of thousands" of requests on social media from 80+ countries in a year (Pocketgamer.biz, 2 Jun 2026).

Academic evidence. Across seven studies, people were less likely to repeat a behaviour after their logged streak broke, even when their actual past behaviour was the same. The effect was stronger when they blamed themselves and weaker when they could repair the streak (Silverman & Barasch, Journal of Consumer Research, 2023).

The way back costs money or currency. Streak Freeze and Streak Repair require gems, "and hence money, if you run out of free gems" (Android Authority, Jun 2026). The returning session also uses energy, which drains as you do exercises (Fig. 2).

Duolingo itself has said friction slows growth. In its Q4 2025 letter, Duolingo said "adding small amounts of friction to encourage more people to subscribe" is "part of the reason our DAU growth has slowed". It decided "to prioritize user growth over monetization" and to reinvest "more than $50M of foregone bookings from friction" into the free experience (Q4 2025 letter). A securities class action filed on 7 Oct 2026 alleges that Energy was designed to create friction for free users. These are untested allegations (InvestmentNews, 8 Oct 2026).

Review mining

Sample. I took the 500 most recent iOS App Store reviews from Apple's public review feed, across seven English-language storefronts (UK 150, US 100, and 50 each from IE, IN, NZ, SG and ZA). They were posted between 7 Sep and 8 Oct 2026, mostly on version 7.142.0. Ratings: 262 five-star, 64 four, 47 three, 43 two, 84 one. I read and coded all 127 one- and two-star reviews by hand. Insights

1. A broken streak is a cliff, not a stumble. Observation: the product is designed around keeping the streak. Losing it is designed as a reset. Evidence: Duolingo calls losing a streak "demotivating". Press reports that streak loss is a common reason people stop returning. Peer-reviewed work shows engagement drops after a break unless the streak can be repaired (all cited above). Why it matters: the "what-the-hell" pattern. Once a goal feels violated, people stop trying. If the product shows "0", it confirms that the effort is gone.

2. Duolingo has already shown how big the comeback audience is. Evidence: 15.4M learners revived their streaks in June 2026, "including nearly 8 million users who had no active streak". The event was named as one of three drivers of Q2 user growth (Q2 2026 letter). The CEO said these users "are also showing better retention than a typical reengaged cohort" (Q2 2026 call). Why it matters: this is not a niche request. Duolingo itself ran it as a growth lever. It was built as a campaign, though, not as a system, and campaigns end.

3. The way back is paid for in the currencies a lapsed free learner is least likely to have. Evidence: repair and freeze cost gems. The comeback session costs energy. Energy is the most-cited single mechanic in 1–2★ reviews (17%). Duolingo's own Q4 2025 letter links friction to slower DAU growth. Why it matters: friction should go where intent is low. A returning user has high intent and little energy, so this is the worst place for friction.

4. Restoring the number is not the same as restoring the skill (Hypothesis). Observation: Revival restored a 72-day streak after three lessons, with no check on what the learner still remembers. Some returning users also find a changed course (4 reviews). Why it matters: if the streak no longer reflects learning, it slowly loses meaning ("streak without learning"). The comeback is the cheapest moment to reconnect the number to real progress.

Benchmark: how others handle a lapse

I compared public store screens. I did not test how each app behaves after a real lapse.

What I take from these. Competitors soften the break (weekly goals, chosen days) or soften the reset (lifetime counters, catch-up review). None of them has Duolingo's scale of streak attachment, so copying a weekly goal outright could weaken what works. The lifetime counter and the catch-up review are worth borrowing.

Options considered

Option User value Business value Effort Risk
A. Repeat Streak Revival every year Medium (only once a year) Proven spike in Q2 2026 Low Teaches users to wait for the event; no help the other 11 months
B. Permanent "Earn it back" path: restore a recently broken streak through a short comeback plan, with an energy-free warm-up High Recovers re-engagement continuously Medium May weaken the urgency of daily practice; may cut gem-based repair revenue
C. Replace the daily streak with a weekly goal (Busuu-style) Medium Unknown; touches the 84% CURR engine High Very high: risks the mechanic behind CURR

Chosen: B. It keeps the daily streak as it is, so CURR is protected. It turns a one-month spike into an always-on system, and it puts friction after the comeback rather than in front of it. The urgency risk is real, so the plan "earns" the streak back over several days rather than offering a free button, and the experiment measures the effect on people whose streaks are still intact (section 8).

Deliberately not building: a one-tap "pay to restore" button with no learning attached. It would make money in the short term, but it would turn the streak into a purchase and confirm the "greedy" theme that already shows up in 17% of negative reviews.

Proposed solution: "Earn it back"

Wireframe-level, screen by screen:

  1. Break moment (next open). Headline: "Your 214-day streak is paused, not gone." Under it: lifetime stats (days learned, words practised), in the style of Speak's lifetime counter. Primary button: "Earn it back in 3 days". Secondary: "Start fresh". It is visible to anyone whose streak of 7+ days broke within the last 14 days (thresholds to be tested).
  2. Comeback plan card on Home. Three checkpoints, to be completed within 7 days. The streak flame shows as greyed "paused 214" rather than "0".
  3. Day 1 warm-up (energy-free). A 3–5 minute review built from the learner's own weakest items before the break, in the style of Speak's Quick Catch-up. Mistakes are labelled "rusty", not "wrong". Duolingo already turns energy off during free trials (Q2 2026 call), so the mechanism exists.
  4. Days 2–3: normal lessons. Normal energy rules apply, which protects the business model.
  5. Restored. "214 days, back on track." In the streak calendar, the paused days show as a distinct "restored" mark, so the count stays honest. Then a prompt to equip a Streak Freeze, which moves protection to the moment users are most motivated to buy it.
  6. Long-lapse variant (30+ days away). First, "What's changed since you left": a course-update summary plus a short placement check. This addresses the "deleted my progress" complaints.

Build note (mobile developer's view)

  • Treat the streak as a server-side state machine: active → at_risk → paused_recoverable → restored | expired. Compute day boundaries on the server, in the learner's own time zone, and make the calculation idempotent. Several reviews describe freezes used "without need" and widgets showing 26 while the app shows 27. Adding new states on top of a counter that users already doubt would make that worse, so fix consistency first.
  • Offline and sync. Lessons finished offline must count toward the comeback checkpoints. Queue the completion events with timestamps, and let the server decide which streak day they belong to.
  • Widgets and notifications. The iOS widget (WidgetKit) and Android widget need to read the new paused state from the same source of truth. Local reminder scheduling has to know about the 7-day plan window.
  • Energy exemption. Pass a session-level flag (energy_exempt: comeback_warmup) from the server rather than adding a client-side rule, so the exemption can be tuned remotely and cannot be exploited by editing the client.
  • What to ship first. Server eligibility, the break screen, the Home card and the energy-free warm-up, all behind a remote-config flag. The "What's changed" screen and placement check come second; they depend on course-diff data I cannot see from outside.
  • Effort (Hypothesis, from my experience with Flutter apps of similar complexity): for an existing team, about 2–3 sprints for phase 1. Most of the work is backend state and analytics, not UI.

Success metrics and validation plan

  • Primary metric: among users whose 7+ day streak breaks, the share active on day 28 after the break. In Duolingo's terms, this is better RURR/SURR and fewer users drifting to dormant.
  • Guardrails: CURR and streak-maintenance rate among users who never broke their streak (so the safety net does not reduce daily practice); Streak Freeze equips and gem spend; subscription conversion; mentions of "streak" and "energy" in 1–2★ reviews; recall accuracy in the warm-up.
  • Experiment design: a user-level A/B test, randomised at the moment of the break (control sees today's experience), staged at 1% → 5% → 25%. Because the guardrail is about behaviour before a break, keep a long-running holdout of users who never see the feature, so any loss of urgency shows up over 8+ weeks.
  • Decision rule (proposed): ship if day-28 retention after a break rises and CURR in the holdout comparison is flat within a pre-set margin.

5-user test script (45 minutes, remote)

Screener: has used Duolingo for 3+ months; had a streak of 30+ days that broke in the last 6 months; mix of free and Super users; at least 2 who did not come back.

  1. Warm-up (5 min). "Tell me about the last time your streak broke. What happened that day? What did you do the next day?"
  2. Feelings (5 min). "What did the zero mean to you?" Listen for self-blame, relief, or "what's the point".
  3. Task 1 (8 min). Show the break-screen prototype. "What would you tap? Why?" Success: they understand "paused" without help.
  4. Task 2 (8 min). "Use the Home screen to see how to get your streak back." Success: they can state the 3 steps and the 7-day window.
  5. Task 3 (8 min). Run the warm-up with 5 real items. Ask: "Did this feel like punishment or a refresher?"
  6. Fairness probe (5 min). "A friend kept their streak without breaking it. Is the restored number fair to them?" Listen for whether the "restored" mark is enough.
  7. Close (6 min). "If this had existed when your streak broke, what would you have done?" Ask them to rank: free restore / earn it back / pay gems / start fresh.

What would change my mind: 3 or more of 5 say a restored streak "doesn't count", or say they would skip practice because a safety net exists.

Risks and open questions (what only the internal team knows)

  • What share of lost DAUs actually follow a streak break, compared with other causes?
  • Does the Revival cohort's better retention last past 60 or 90 days, or was it a novelty effect?
  • How much revenue comes from gem-based Streak Repair, and how much would "Earn it back" cannibalise it?
  • Public reports disagree on the Revival rules: some say users needed no active streak (Android Police), but the letter's "nearly 8 million… had no active streak" implies others did. How were existing streaks handled?
  • Is there already a repair window or an "earn back" test running that public sources do not show?

LinkedIn spin-offs (STOP formula)

  1. Tension + Proof + Open loop: "Duolingo gave 15.4 million people their lost streak back. Then the offer expired after 30 days." Visual: the Revival screen (Fig. 3) with a red circle on "13 days left".
  2. Sides + Tension: "Streak apps are designed for the day you keep going. Almost none are designed for the day you stop." Visual: split screen with ❌ "0" next to ✓ "Paused 214 · 1,123 lessons lifetime".
  3. Proof + Open loop: "Duolingo told investors that friction slowed its growth. I found the moment where friction hurts most." Visual: the energy counter at 25→24→23→22 (Fig. 2), with a lime arrow pointing to an energy-free comeback screen.
