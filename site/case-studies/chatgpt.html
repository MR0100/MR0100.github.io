ChatGPT shows free users its best features, then hides the meter

Disclaimer. This is an independent study by Mitul Vaghasiya. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or based on any internal data from OpenAI. It uses only public sources, linked inline. Where I give my own judgement, I write "I". Anything I could not verify is labelled Hypothesis.

I started out to study discoverability: how much ChatGPT's empty text box hides. The public evidence pointed somewhere else. In a sample of 176 recent 1–3★ App Store reviews, only 1 complained about not finding a feature. 54 complained about usage limits and upgrade prompts, and 21 of those were about photos and images, the same features the App Store page uses to sell the app. People find the features. The trouble starts when they hit a limit they couldn't see coming, in the middle of a task. I suggest a visible allowance and a gentler landing at the limit, and I set out how to test that with five users and a staged rollout.

Why this app

  • Scale. OpenAI reported 900 million weekly active users and 50 million paying subscribers in February 2026 (TechCrunch, 27 Feb 2026; OpenAI). Sensor Tower says ChatGPT became "the fastest app to reach 1 billion global downloads across iOS and Google Play" in July 2025 (Sensor Tower, Jul 2025). Google Play shows 1B+ downloads (Google Play, checked 10 Oct 2026). The US iOS listing has a 4.83 average from about 11.1 million ratings (iTunes lookup API, 10 Oct 2026).
  • Business model. Freemium plus ads. Paid tiers are Go, Plus and Pro. In the US, Go costs $8 a month, Plus $20 and Pro $200 (OpenAI, Introducing ChatGPT Go; TechCrunch). Since 9 Feb 2026, US users on Free and Go see ads (MacRumors, 9 Feb 2026). Appfigures estimates mobile consumer spending at $2.48B in 2025, up from $487M in 2024 (TechCrunch, 18 Dec 2025).
  • Retention is already strong. Sensor Tower data, as reported by Apoorv Agrawal, puts ChatGPT's week-4 retention at about 66%, against 44% for Gemini and 41% for Claude, and DAU:MAU at 45% (Tailwinds, 10 Mar 2026).
  • The metric that matters here is conversion without losing trust. On 6 Aug 2026 OpenAI made text chat unlimited for Free users and said limits still apply to file uploads, images and other tools (Yahoo Tech, 6 Aug 2026; PYMNTS). So the tools now separate Free from paid, and the moment a Free user hits a tool limit is where the upgrade case is made or lost. Free users also earn ad revenue now, so a frustrated free user costs money even if they never upgrade.

The problem

Who is affected: Free (and Go) users on mobile who use the camera, photo upload or image creation. Those are the features that make the app feel worth installing.

What goes wrong: Text is unlimited, but tools are metered. In my reading of the sources, the app tells users about a tool limit when they hit it, not before. One third-party guide, checked against OpenAI's help pages, says ChatGPT "does not currently show how much of your rolling upload-rate quota has been used or how much remains" (izzedo, updated 1 Oct 2026; same wording in yingtu.ai, updated 30 Sep 2026). OpenAI publishes no fixed daily image number for Free, and limits "may vary based on system conditions" (same sources). I could not open OpenAI's help centre directly (HTTP 403), so this point rests on secondary sources.

Review mining

  • Sample. The 1,000 most recent App Store reviews from the public RSS feed across five English-language storefronts: US, UK, India, Canada and Australia (feed example). They are dated 19 Sep to 8 Oct 2026. 176 are 1–3★, and I coded 147 of those as having a usable complaint. The other 29 were off-topic (environment protests, abuse, gibberish) or too short to code.

Company view. ChatGPT's head, Nick Turley, has said the product is "really powerful, but it's lacking something very important, which is affordances," and compared it to "this appliance that nobody quite knew everything you could do with it" (TechCrunch, 8 Oct 2025). My original angle was right that there is a gap in affordances. The reviews suggest the gap that hurts most is this: users can't see how much of a feature they have left.

Insights

1. The leak comes after discovery.

  • Observation: Users find the photo and image features. They get angry when those features stop working.
  • Evidence: 1 discoverability complaint against 54 about limits, 21 of them about images (Section 2).
  • Why it matters: An onboarding tour would push more people into the same wall faster. Hooked-style habit loops need a reward that comes reliably; a reward that stops without warning undercuts the habit.

2. "Unlimited" changed the deal, but the UI didn't.

  • Observation: Since August 2026, Free has two kinds of limit: unlimited text and metered tools. To most users it is all one chat box.
  • Evidence: The 6 Aug 2026 announcement (Yahoo Tech); the "unlimited chats" review above.
  • Why it matters: Expectation violation. When a promise is broken, people react more strongly to the loss than they would have to a smaller promise that was kept (loss aversion).

3. The wall comes at peak intent, so it feels like punishment, not an offer.

  • Observation: The upgrade pitch appears when the user is blocked, mid-task, often with a multi-hour wait.
  • Evidence: 20 of the 54 limit reviews mention waiting or reset times; 22 mention upgrade or pay prompts.
  • Why it matters: Peak-end rule: people remember a session by its worst moment and its end. Here both are the wall. Repeated prompts trigger reactance, so the user pushes back instead of considering the offer.

4. Free users are now customers too.

  • Observation: With ads on Free and Go (MacRumors), a free user who leaves annoyed costs revenue even if they never upgrade.
  • Why it matters: The limit moment should be designed for two good outcomes, upgrading or coming back tomorrow, not just one.

What I take from this: Gemini's mode chip is the clearest pattern for showing what you can do. It names the tool inside the composer, so it is also the natural place to show how much of that tool is left. None of the four makes the remaining allowance visible on a Free mobile account, as far as I can verify. That's a gap ChatGPT could close first.

Options considered

Option User value Business value Effort Risk
A. Feature tour or onboarding carousel (my starting angle) Low to medium: users already find the features Low: sends more people to the wall faster Low Adds friction before first value
B. Visible allowance + soft landing at the limit (chosen) High: predictable, fair, keeps the task alive Upgrade offered at a planned moment; brings users back for ads Medium Showing the meter could make some users ration their use (needs testing)
C. Raise Free tool limits High Compute cost; weaker reason to upgrade Business decision Not a design lever

Why B: it fixes the moment the reviews describe, keeps OpenAI's pricing intact, and fits a mobile client.

What I would deliberately not build: a full-screen hard paywall or a countdown timer at the limit. It turns peak intent into pressure, and pressure is what the "upgrade, upgrade, upgrade" reviews are complaining about.

Proposed solution (wireframe level)

  1. Tool chips with a quiet allowance. When a user picks Camera, Photo or Create image from "+", a chip appears in the composer (the Gemini pattern), for example "Create image · 2 left today". When exact numbers aren't stable, show a band instead: "a few left", "last one today".
  2. Warning before the last use. One step before the cap, a single line above the composer: "Last image for today, resets at 3:40 pm. Make it count?" There is no modal.
  3. Soft landing at the limit. A bottom sheet with three equal choices: - Save for later. The request is kept and runs at reset, with a notification when it's done. - Do it in text instead. Text is unlimited, for example "describe the edit" or "translate from typed text". - Upgrade. One line on what Go or Plus adds for this tool, with the price.

The draft and photo stay attached whatever the user chooses.

  1. Fair failures. If a generation fails or is refused, say whether it counted. Hypothesis: it should not count.
  2. A "Today" row in Settings → Plan. One line per tool with what is left and when it resets. It's a single place to check, not a dashboard.

Build note (mobile developer's view).

  • Server-driven state. Limits are dynamic, so the client should never hard-code them. Render a server-sent allowance object (tool, remaining or band, resetsAt, countsFailures), with every string coming from the server so copy can be A/B tested without an app release.
  • Native or Flutter. It's the same model either way: one allowance stream per session, refreshed after each tool call.
  • Notifications. "Save for later" needs push permission. Ask for it in the sheet, where the reason is obvious, not at first launch. iOS provisional authorization can deliver quietly before the full prompt.
  • Running the saved request. Run it on the server so it works across devices. Use idempotency keys so a retry can't spend the allowance twice.
  • What to ship first. The pre-limit warning line and the three-choice sheet, because they need almost no new backend. Then chips with counts. Then save-for-later.
  • Effort, my estimate. The client UI is about 2–3 sprints for one mobile team; the backend allowance API is the real dependency.

Success metrics and validation plan

  • Primary metric: 7-day return rate of Free users after their first tool limit.
  • Secondary metrics:
  • Upgrade conversion within 7 days of a limit event.
  • Share of limit events that end in "save for later" or "text instead" rather than leaving the app.
  • Guardrails:
  • Overall free-to-paid conversion must not drop.
  • Tool uses per Free user must not fall sharply, as a check on rationing.
  • Ad impressions per session must hold.
  • Support contacts about limits.
  • Share of 1–3★ reviews tagged "limits", re-coded with this method.

Experiment design.

  • Phase 1: a 5-user test on a clickable prototype (below).
  • Phase 2: an A/B test on Free users in one market with three arms:
  • control;
  • warning line plus sheet;
  • everything, including chips and save-for-later.

Run it for 2–4 weeks, randomise by account, and report by tool.

  • Phase 3: a staged rollout by storefront, with a 5% holdout kept for a month.

5-user test script (about 30 min each, remote, on their own phone).

  • Recruit: five people who have used ChatGPT Free on mobile in the last month and have never paid. Mix of countries and ages, and at least two who use photos or images weekly.
  1. Warm-up (3 min). "Tell me about the last time you used ChatGPT on your phone. What did you use it for?"
  2. Expectation check (2 min). "On the free plan, what do you think is limited, and what isn't?" Note any mention of "unlimited".
  3. Task 1 (5 min). "Translate this menu photo." The prototype uses one allowance. Ask: "How many more could you do today? How sure are you?"
  4. Task 2 (5 min). "Now edit this selfie into a headshot." The warning line appears. Ask: "What does this tell you? What would you do now?"
  5. Task 3 (7 min). "Edit one more photo." The limit sheet appears. Think aloud. Which option did they pick, and why? Did they notice that the draft was kept?
  6. Fairness probe (3 min). Show a failed generation. "Do you think that counted? Should it?"
  7. Wrap-up (5 min). "Rate how fair the limit felt, 1–5. Would you come back tomorrow for this? What would make you pay?"
  • Pass signal: at least 4 of 5 predict their remaining allowance correctly after Task 2, and no one describes the sheet as "pushy". Write findings as quotes plus counts, not opinions.

Risks and open questions (only OpenAI would know)

  • How limits are really computed: per tool, rolling or fixed window, and adjusted by load. Is an exact or banded count even promisable?
  • Whether failed or refused generations count today. One review claims they do.
  • Whether showing the meter increases or reduces tool use and upgrades. Rationing is a real risk.
  • How limit events, ad revenue and upgrades trade off in OpenAI's own funnel data.
  • Whether these reviews reflect A/B cohorts, regional limits or a temporary tightening. One report says free image limits were cut without notice (aiweekly, not fully verified).
  • What the real limit screen looks like on 1.2026.272. I haven't captured it yet.

LinkedIn spin-offs (STOP formula)

  1. Proof + Open loop: "I read 176 angry ChatGPT reviews. 1 was about finding features. 54 were about something else." Visual: two bars, 1 vs 54, with the 54 in red.
  2. Tension + Sides: "ChatGPT tells free users chat is unlimited. Then a photo edit says: come back in 18 hours." (The 18 hours is one reviewer's report; say so in the body.) Visual: the App Store "Edit your look" screen next to a redrawn limit sheet, with one red circle on the reset time.
  3. Sides + Tension (request-I-hear pattern): "'We need a better onboarding tour.' Founders ask me this every month. ChatGPT's reviews show where users actually leave." Visual: a ❌ tour carousel vs a ✓ "2 left today" chip.

Each post should end with the fix, the three-choice sheet, and a question that asks for reasons: "Would you show the meter, or hide it? Why?"
